01 / The research

Ecological connectivity of genomic markers of antimicrobial resistance in Escherichia coli in Hong Kong. Nature Communications (2025).

The study describes 1,016 isolate genomes from 63 aquatic sites. “Human”, “Animal” and “Environment” are ecological source categories, not directly sampled patient, animal or population cohorts.

Read the paper ↗

02 / The table used here

revused-plasmid-ARG.txt, released by the authors. This snapshot contains 3,522 unique plasmid IDs and 942 distinct isolate IDs. It is a specific released table, not a complete listing of every isolate in the paper.

03 / How the chart works

Count each plasmid record once if it contains the exact selected marker token. Divide by all plasmid records in that source category under the selected recovery filter. Repeated marker annotations in one record count once. One record has no source category; it remains in the full table and overall total, and is excluded from the three category bars.

“Without antibiotic selection” filters the authors’ Selection field to N. This is laboratory recovery without antibiotic selection. It does not describe a person’s antibiotic use. The selected and non-selected samples are not random, matched populations.

04 / Reading the specimen cards

All three illustrative records contain tet(A), have circular assemblies and were recovered without antibiotic selection. Plasmid mobility is predicted from sequence features using Plascad. The card does not report an observed transfer event. The ring is a schematic marker inventory, not a gene-position map.

A shared marker does not establish identical plasmids, a direction of transmission, human exposure or clinical risk. We have not reproduced the paper’s sequence-level analyses or performed new laboratory work.

05 / What to test with the team

Can a visitor explain the difference between a shared marker and proven transmission? Does changing the recovery filter help them notice sampling bias? Which follow-up would a domain expert actually prioritise?

This prototype uses published tables and deterministic calculations. It contains no trained prediction model or live surveillance feed. The landscape is illustrative and does not plot sample coordinates.
